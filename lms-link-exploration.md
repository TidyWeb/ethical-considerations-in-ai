## 1. Moral Machine

- LMS destination: [Moral Machine](https://www.moralmachine.net/)
- Opened destination: the live Moral Machine welcome page. It describes a platform for collecting human judgments about machine decisions, especially driverless-car dilemmas, and offers Judge, Design and Browse interfaces.
- Explored the site's own **Instructions** panel. Judge presents scenarios and asks visitors to select a preferred outcome; the site's stated outcomes can involve death, injury or uncertainty. Design lets visitors construct scenarios. Browse lets visitors inspect scenarios created by users and see their textual outcome summaries.
- The LMS lesson frames the site as a way to encounter moral dilemmas. The companion treats its responses as evidence of how participants judge constructed scenarios, not a definitive moral answer or a complete public mandate.
- An embedded explainer, “Moral Machine — Human Perspectives on Machine Ethics”, was identified. Its YouTube transcript endpoint reported that no transcript is available. The video was not played. No judgment, scenario, like or comment was submitted.
- The instructions panel describes the site's interactions; this capture did not enter those interactions or inspect user-generated scenario records.

## 2. Lethal Autonomous Weapons Pledge

- LMS destination: [Future of Life Institute pledge](https://futureoflife.org/2018/06/05/lethal-autonomous-weapons-pledge/)
- The LMS URL now redirects to the canonical page, [Lethal Autonomous Weapons Pledge](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/lethal-autonomous-weapons-pledge/).
- The live page is dated **6 June 2018**. It argues that decisions to take human life should not be delegated to machines, citing moral responsibility and risks of destabilisation, oppression and arms races. Signatories pledge not to participate in or support the development, manufacture, trade or use of lethal autonomous weapons, and call for international norms, regulation and laws.
- The page separately reports that 30 UN member states had endorsed a call for a ban. That and the signature totals are page-reported figures, not independently verified here; do not present them as a current legal status.
- A name-entry form is present. It was not filled in or submitted. The page is a 2018 pledge, not itself a treaty or a statement of current law.

## 3. UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

- LMS destination: [UNESCO article](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence)
- The page identifies the Recommendation as UNESCO's first global standard on AI ethics, adopted in 2021 and applicable to UNESCO's 194 member states. The page says its first publication was 16 May 2023 and its last update was 26 September 2024.
- Its summary foregrounds human rights and dignity, transparency, fairness, human oversight and policy action areas including data governance, environment, gender, education, research, health and social wellbeing.
- This article is a short overview. It links to the underlying Recommendation and a broader UNESCO topic page; no assertion in the article makes it a binding domestic law in every jurisdiction.

## 4. European Parliament AI Act explainer

- LMS destination: [EU AI Act: first regulation on artificial intelligence](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/topics/en/article/20230601STO93804/eu-ai-act-first-regulation-on-artificial-intelligence)
- The page opened successfully. It is dated **8 June 2023**, last updated **19 February 2025**. It explains a risk-based structure, prohibited practices, high-risk system obligations, transparency duties, human oversight and a compliance timeline as described at that time.
- Its timeline still says the Act would be fully applicable 24 months after entry into force and high-risk rules would apply after 36 months. Because this explainer predates later amendments and current implementation updates, do not use its schedule as the current legal timetable. The overview uses the separate, dated official current-law research in `primary-research.md` instead.

## 5. UK Government AI ethics and safety guidance

- LMS destination: [Understanding artificial intelligence ethics and safety](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/understanding-artificial-intelligence-ethics-and-safety#understanding-what-ai-ethics-is)
- The page opened at the requested section. It is government guidance published **10 June 2019**. It describes three routes to unintended harm: misuse, questionable design, and unintended negative consequences. It advises project teams to tailor governance to social and ethical impact.
- Its framework names four SUM values (respect, connect, care, protect) and four FAST Track principles (fairness, accountability, sustainability, transparency), then recommends process-based governance across the AI lifecycle. It gives fairness dimensions including data, design, outcomes and implementation, and calls for responsibility, monitoring, explanation and auditability.
- The page points to The Alan Turing Institute's full guidance, the Data Ethics Framework and related AI project guides. These are further reading links on the linked GOV.UK page; the LMS's five direct content links were the capture boundary for this pass.
- This 2019 page is useful as a historical practical framework, but its publication date and framework names should remain visible. It does not replace current legal or regulator guidance.
