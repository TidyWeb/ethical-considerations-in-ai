# Ethical considerations in Artificial Intelligence — analysis and enrichment

## Learner route

The companion now follows all ten Code Institute sections but supplies the explanations the short lesson leaves open. Its teaching route is: distinguish morals, ethics, law and policy; make the eight roboethics principles usable; reason through dilemmas; compare work, care and military robotics; understand automated decisions and personal data; then connect ethical aims to accountable governance, risk controls, practice and transfer. Section 02 now expands the separate OECD values alongside the LMS principles; section 08 explains the OECD’s five government recommendations alongside operational NIST risk management; section 10 ends with an OECD-informed student checklist for planning a data project.

The guide keeps the lesson's original topics visible and labels independent research and invented scenarios. Links appear after the idea they support. The LMS capture remains separate so later legal updates do not alter the source record.

## Research questions and wording controls

- The LMS contains historical law and policy statements. Preserve them as source material; do not repeat them as current status without checking current official sources.
- Do not say a robot, developer, manufacturer or user is automatically liable in every case. Responsibility and legal liability depend on the law, role, jurisdiction and facts.
- Do not turn Asimov's fictional laws or the LMS roboethics principles into binding law or an implementable safety specification.
- Do not use “human in the loop” as proof of meaningful control. Show information, time, competence, authority, workload and ability to intervene or reverse.
- Do not treat a public-preference survey as a vote that settles rights or policy. Explain participant selection and scenario framing.
- Do not treat accuracy, fairness or safety as a single score. Identify the outcome, error types, affected groups, use context and impact.
- Do not conflate personal-data processing, profiling, solely automated significant decisions, and EU AI Act high-risk classification; they answer different questions.
- Data protection is broader than AI and broader than automated-decision safeguards. A human decision can still process personal data; an automatic rule can be relevant even if it is not AI.
- The UK legal summary is specifically dated 14 October 2026. It is learning context, not advice on an individual case. The ICO practical guidance is under review following DUAA.

## NIST AI RMF extraction notes

The learner guide summarises the source sections directly rather than leaving them as pointers. Risk framing covers likelihood and consequence, positive and negative impact, affected people and environment, uncertainty, metric limits, third-party risks, lab-to-field differences, prioritisation and residual risk. The audience section explains lifecycle roles and the importance of affected communities and other outside perspectives. Trustworthiness is explained through all seven NIST characteristics and practical trade-offs. Effectiveness is treated as something organisations should evaluate, not a certification. The Core section explains Govern, Map, Measure and Manage and how they connect, including go/no-go decisions, documented limits, tests, risk responses, alternatives, appeals, monitoring and safe shutdown.

The student checklist translates this into beginner actions on purpose, alternatives, affected people, provenance and permissions, sensitivity and re-identification, coverage, measurement quality, bias, harm, security/retention, review, documentation and stop conditions. It is a planning aid, not legal advice or a substitute for tutor, institutional or professional approvals.

## Teaching journey and change made

The earlier overview named values and principles but moved too quickly to links. This revision replaces the abstract section-two headline, lists Asimov’s Three Laws and later Zeroth Law, and extracts the NIST framework into teachable parts instead of treating it as one citation. The rebuild now gives each major concept an ordinary-language foothold, a concrete scenario, the relevant distinction, an explanation of what can go wrong, and an applied question before its sources. Section 06 starts with a fictional automatic loan refusal, defines the terms and scope, compares adjacent concepts, walks the learner through checks and safeguards, then gives the official links. The rest of the lesson route receives the same treatment at a proportionate depth.

The risk process is a continuing loop: **purpose → affected people and context → evidence and uncertainty → controls and owners → limited use → monitoring and response**. It can return to earlier steps, narrow the use or stop it. The guide keeps the central hiring case stable and explicitly flags when it switches to robotics, credit, work, military or retail examples.

## OECD enrichment mapping

The OECD Recommendation was amended on 3 May 2024. Its values-based principles speak to AI actors and set out outcomes such as inclusive benefit, human rights, transparency, robustness and accountability. Its separate recommendations are directed to governments: invest in interdisciplinary AI research and representative privacy-respecting open data; build an inclusive ecosystem for data, tools, connectivity and knowledge sharing; create an adaptable and interoperable policy environment with controlled experimentation; build skills and support a fair labour transition; and cooperate internationally through shared knowledge, standards and indicators. These are policy recommendations, not direct legal obligations for an individual student.

The guide explains each of the five values with concrete project questions and examples, applies them to the fictional hiring ranker, and separately translates each government recommendation into a question a student can investigate (evidence and dataset quality; access and dependencies; rules and bounded pilots; worker voice and skills; and transfer between populations or jurisdictions). The expanded checklist also asks about environmental and work impacts, affected people's input, named owners and stop authority, and transfer limits. It does not claim a student can supply the public policy conditions governments are asked to create.

Sources: [OECD AI Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/ai-principles.html); [OECD Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence, OECD/LEGAL/0449](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0449). Current official source checked 14 October 2026.
